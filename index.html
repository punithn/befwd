    Logo

    The wordmark holds one idea in one word: “be” is heavy, upright and rooted; “fwd” is light and leans into what’s next. It stands alone, in one colour at a time, never in a container.

    Logo and clearspace

    Hover the logo to see its clearspace. Keep free space equal to the margin shown on every side. The margin is one sixth of the logo height, rounded to a multiple of 4: 20px at 120px high, 16px at 96px high, 12px at 72px high and 8px at 48px high. Nothing may enter this zone.     Accent colors

    Soot and charcoal step up from ink for layered dark surfaces. Don’t mix them within one panel.

    Weights & sizing

    One scale for every surface. Each level has a fixed typeface, weight and size.

    H1 · Bricolage Grotesque · Bold 700 · 36px / 1.15
    Walls that breathe

    Sets the page title or one big statement. Use it once per layout.

    H2 · Bricolage Grotesque · Medium 500 · 28px / 1.2
    What the courtyard knew about heat

    Opens a major section, such as a chapter or a dispatch.

    H3 · Inter · Bold 700 · 20px / 1.35
    Lime plaster takes on moisture and gives it back

    Introduces a sub-section or a key message under a heading.

    Body · Inter · Regular 400 · 16px / 1.6
    No one built a thick wall by accident. Each layer answered a climate, a craft and a community that knew how to wait.

    All running text: descriptions, captions and paragraphs. Keep lines to about 60 characters.

    Patterns

    Everything starts from one triangle, turned to face back and forward. One lattice that repeats endlessly and bends easily into other shapes.

    Download pattern files ↓

    Primary pattern and variation

    Equilateral triangles in a continuous lattice. A triangle facing right (▶) looks forward; one facing left (◀) looks back. Each is the other’s mirror, and neither exists without the other. The primary pattern is the lattice itself; the variation keeps the same triangle and the same angles, with a different emphasis.

    Tone of Voice

    We sound like a good salon host: observant, unhurried, generous with the question. Telegraphic, never terse; precise, never gatekeeping.

    Download voice summary ↓

    Principles

    Each principle has a one-line rule and a before and after. Copy the “we write” side.

    One voice

    Observant, unhurried, generous with the question.

    We write the way a good salon host talks: briefly, precisely, and with a question left open. Three principles keep us there.

    Intellectual and grounded

    Thinks in ideas, stays in the material.

    Instead of Obsessed with this! We write Lime breathes. Cement doesn’t.
    Telegraphic and clear

    Short lines, exact words.

    Instead of Let’s dive deep We write Density, with shade.
    Exploratory, not preachy

    Asks, rather than tells.

    Instead of Game-changer We write What did your city’s walls know that its towers forgot?

    Title and body tone

    Titles and body copy share a voice but not a job. A title makes one point. The body explains why.

    Titles

    Walls that breathe
    • One idea
    • Under seven words
    • Sentence case, no exclamation marks

    Body

    No one built a thick wall by accident. Each layer answered a climate, a craft and a community that knew how to wait.
    • Telegraphic, never terse
    • Specific nouns over adjectives
    • Reason first, then detail
    Hyped

    No “game-changer”, “obsessed” or “level up”.

    Chatty

    No “Happy Monday, fam!” Warm is not casual.

    Every post

    Ends with an open question worth debating, never “drop an emoji below”, and a clean pointer to the full dispatch on befwd.substack.com.
